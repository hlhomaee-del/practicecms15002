Primary vs Secondary Insurance

Learn how to determine payer order — and why guessing can delay or deny the entire claim.

🧠 Prior Knowledge: A patient has insurance through their own employer and also through their spouse's employer. Which is typically primary?

COB Rules You Need to Know

Subscriber Rule

When a person has coverage as both a subscriber and a dependent, their own plan (subscriber) is primary.

Birthday Rule

For dependent children covered under both parents' plans: the parent whose birthday falls earlier in the calendar year is primary for the child.

Active vs COBRA

An active employee plan is generally primary over COBRA continuation coverage.

Medicare Secondary

When a patient has employer coverage AND Medicare, the employer plan is typically primary if the employer has 20+ employees (Medicare Secondary Payer rules).

A 10-year-old child is covered under both parents' plans. Mom's birthday is March 15. Dad's birthday is January 22. Whose plan is primary for the child?

🧠 Billing Mindset

Always determine payer order at intake. Verify both cards, ask about other coverage, and apply COB rules before submitting. Fixing payer order after submission wastes weeks.

Workers' Compensation Logic

When the injury is work-related, the entire billing workflow changes.

What Changes with Workers' Comp?

Different Payer

Workers' comp is billed to the workers' compensation carrier — NOT the patient's regular health insurance.

No Patient Responsibility

Generally, the patient does NOT pay copays, deductibles, or coinsurance for approved work-related injuries.

Claim Form Changes

Item 10a must indicate the condition is employment-related. The WC carrier goes in the payer field. Employer information may be required.

Authorization Often Required

Many WC carriers require pre-authorization for treatment. The claim number from the WC carrier is essential.

⚠ Critical Verification Step

Before billing workers' comp, verify: 1) Is this truly a work-related injury? 2) Does the employer have a WC policy? 3) What is the WC claim number? 4) Is authorization required? Billing the wrong payer delays everything.

After Primary Has Paid

Learn what changes on the claim form and in your thinking when the primary insurance has already adjudicated.

What Happens After Primary Adjudication?

When the primary insurance processes the claim, several things change for the secondary submission:

You Now Have an EOB
The primary EOB shows: allowed amount, what was paid, what was applied to deductible, contractual adjustments, and remaining patient responsibility.
Item 29 — Amount Paid
On the secondary claim, Item 29 should reflect the amount the primary insurance already paid.
Attach the Primary EOB
The secondary payer needs the primary EOB to coordinate benefits correctly. Without it, the claim will likely be rejected or delayed.
The Secondary Processes Differently
The secondary payer adjudicates based on what the primary already paid. They will NOT re-adjudicate from scratch — they consider what remains.

Key Formula

Contractual Write-off = Billed − Allowed
Applied to Deductible = Unmet deductible amount (from allowed)
Insurance Pays = (Allowed − Deductible Applied) × Insurance %
Patient Coinsurance = (Allowed − Deductible Applied) × Patient %
Total Patient Responsibility = Deductible Applied + Coinsurance + Copay
